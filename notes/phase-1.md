# Phase 1 — Define the Portfolio Before Coding

## 1.1 Purpose

1. Who is this portfolio for?

Primarily software engineering recruiters and hiring managers evaluating me for co-op/internship opportunities, but also technical interviewers and developers who want to understand how I actually build software.


2. What should a recruiter understand within 10 seconds?

I am a Computer Science student who builds substantial software rather than only completing coursework. I have experience across full-stack development, backend systems, AI-assisted development, and software architecture, and I like building ambitious projects and experimenting with unusual technical ideas.


3. What should a technical interviewer understand after 2 minutes?

I want them to understand that I know more than individual programming languages and frameworks. I understand how complete systems fit together — APIs, databases, asynchronous processing, background workers, concurrency, testing, CI/CD, architecture, and deployment. I want my projects to demonstrate that I can make engineering decisions, take ownership of systems, and think about performance, scalability, and maintainability. I also want to demonstrate that I can use AI coding tools like Claude Code as part of a serious development workflow while still understanding, reviewing, testing, and owning the code.


4. What should a developer understand after inspecting your projects?

I want my repositories to show that I can structure and maintain real applications, not just make something that works. They should demonstrate clear architecture, separation of concerns, readable code, testing, documentation, Git practices, and thoughtful engineering decisions. I want someone to be able to inspect a project and understand why I made certain technical decisions, not just see a list of technologies.


5. What makes this portfolio different from a generic CS-student portfolio?

I don't want it to feel like a resume turned into a website. I want it to be an interactive representation of how I think, what I build, and what I am interested in. The visual identity will be influenced by anime, deathcore, drawing, the gym, and other interests that are genuinely part of me, while still remaining professional. More importantly, the portfolio will show both what I have already built and what I want to build through an Experimental Lab containing ambitious ideas involving audio analysis, AI, developer tools, computer vision, networking, productivity, and adaptive software. I want visitors to leave understanding not just what technologies I know, but what kinds of problems I am curious about and what I might build next.


Positioning statement:

I'm a Computer Science student and software developer who likes building ambitious systems, understanding how they work under the hood, and experimenting with ideas that sit outside the usual student-project box.

## 1.3 User journey

For each step, write what you want the visitor to think:

**Landing** → "This is clearly a software engineer, and this portfolio is different from the usual student portfolio. I want to see more."

**Understand who you are** → "I understand who Prashaant is beyond his resume. He is a Computer Science student who likes building things, learning deeply, and has interests outside of software that actually influence his personality and creativity."

**See technical credibility** → "He isn't just listing technologies. He has actually worked with real systems, understands architecture, and can build beyond simple student projects."

**Explore projects** → "These projects show how he thinks and solves problems. He understands the technical decisions behind what he builds, and some of his ideas are genuinely ambitious and unusual."

**See personality** → "I have a sense of who this person actually is. His interests in anime, music, drawing, gym, and experimentation make him memorable rather than feeling like another generic CS student."

**Contact you** → "He is someone I could realistically see working with. I know what he does, what he is looking for, and how to get in touch with him."
